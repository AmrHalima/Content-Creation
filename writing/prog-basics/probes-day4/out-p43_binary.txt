== 43.3  worst-case probes as n grows, measured by brute force ==
n           | measured worst probes | ceil(log2 n) | ceil(log2(n+1)) | log2(n)
5           |                     3 |              3 |                3 | 2.322
8           |                     4 |              3 |                4 | 3.000
16          |                     5 |              4 |                5 | 4.000
1000        |                    10 |             10 |               10 | 9.966
1023        |                    10 |             10 |               10 | 9.999
1024        |                    11 |             10 |               11 | 10.000
1000000     |                    20 |             20 |               20 | 19.932
1000000000  |                    30 |             30 |               30 | 29.897
=> the closed form that MATCHES the measured counts is ceil(log2(n+1)), not ceil(log2 n).
   They differ exactly at powers of two: n=8 -> 4 probes but ceil(log2 8) = 3;
   n=1024 -> 11 probes but ceil(log2 1024) = 10. 1000 is not a power of two, so the CTA's
   answer is 10 either way - lucky, but do not let that teach the wrong formula.

== 43.4  the two CTAs, answered with the numbers above ==
Hook: 'Find one number among a billion in about 30 steps.'
  log2(1,000,000,000) = 29.8974 -> worst case 30 probes.
  2^30 = 1073741824 > 1,000,000,000, so 30 halvings is enough. 'about 30' is TRUE,
  and 30 is the worst case, not an average.
CTA: 'about how many steps for 1,000 items?'
  measured worst case over all 1,000 values plus both miss cases = 10 probes.
  ceil(log2(1000)) = 10  (log2(1000) = 9.9658)
  best case = 1 probe (target happens to be the first middle).
  pinned answer for the comment: 10.
  2^10 = 1024 >= 1000, which is why 10 is the number and not 9.
