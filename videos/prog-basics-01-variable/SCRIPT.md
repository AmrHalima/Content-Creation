# SCRIPT — prog-basics-01-variable (EN master)

**Voice:** Kokoro `af_heart`, speed 0.74, local and offline, pinned by `studio voice` from
`BRIEF.md` (`tts_provider: kokoro`, `voice: af_heart`). ElevenLabs is unusable on this machine
(`.agents/design.md` voice_id empty, free-tier keys return HTTP 402) and was not substituted.
HeyGen not used. No BGM, no SFX (bible section 7).

**Voice direction:** One person explaining something to a friend who has never coded, at a whiteboard.
Warm and quick, never careful. The only emphasis in the whole part is on **stays** in line 3 and on
**fifteen** in line 4. No teacher lift at the end of sentences. The CTA line is spoken like a
question you actually want answered.

**Budget:** 91 spoken words / 5 lines. At the campaign's 2.0 words per finished second that is ~45s
against a 52s ceiling. Real durations come from `studio cues` timestamps and win.

---

## Line 1 — Hook (Frame 1)

**Time:** 0.00 — 6.5s (estimate; real VO wins)
**Delivery:** Conversational, no build-up. "remembers" gets the small lift; the second sentence
starts without a gap.

    Every app you use remembers things. Here's the trick behind it.

## Line 2 — The box (Frame 2)

**Time:** 6.5 — 16.0s
**Delivery** Slower than it feels. "labeled box" is the keyword: the chamber draws on exactly those
two words. The three instructions ("put… give… use…") run evenly, no list-voice.

    A variable is a labeled box. Put a value inside, give the box a name, and use that name whenever
    you need the value.

## Line 3 — The name stays (Frame 3)

**Time:** 16.0 — 25.0s
**Delivery:** Land "stays", then leave a real beat before "the name is not the value", which is said
quietly and flat. This is the sentence the part exists for.

    Change what is inside and the name stays the same. That is the trick: the name is not the value.

## Line 4 — The example (Frame 4)

**Time:** 25.0 — 37.0s
**Delivery:** Read it as arithmetic, not as code: "score becomes score plus five", never "equals".
"Fifteen" is the payoff, so it is not rushed.

    Score starts at ten. Then score becomes score plus five. Print it, and you get fifteen.

## Line 5 — The quiz (Frame 5)

**Time:** 37.0 — 45.0s
**Delivery:** The question rises at the end; the instruction "drop the number below" is flat and
short, and then out. No sign-off, no "thanks for watching".

    So: if score starts at one hundred, and you add five, what prints? Drop the number below.
